      Worst wind

      If the wind is variable, this uses the least helpful heading in that range: the most tailwind, or the smallest headwind if it never becomes a tailwind (if a range of wind directions is given).

      Gust or MAX is used when it is stronger than the mean.

      T = tailwind, H = headwind, X = crosswind, in knots. The direction shown is that WORST heading, not necessarily the mean on the ATIS.

      When the displayed ATIS is stale, the strip uses the on-screen METAR if that report has wind. METAR and TAF degrees are true; GearUp shows an estimated magnetic heading from a dated variation table and uses that magnetic value for the stale strip.

      TSAT EOBT slots

      EOBT is Estimated Off Blocks Time: The flight plan time for the flight to be ready for departure. ATC usually allow a tolerance of +/- 15 minutes.

      TOBT, Target Off Blocks Time, is a more accurate measure, usually within +/- 5 minutes, which tells the local CDM system and NMOC Brussels when the airline believes a flight will be ready.

      From this, a TTOT is calculated (Target Take Off Time), which may be adjusted to allow for departure separations, CTOT etc, and then working backwards will provide a TSAT (Target Start Approval Time) which will provide both ATC and the airline the most suitable start up time given all the factors that the local CDM system takes into account.

      • EOBT: Estimated Off-Block Time This is the estimated time at which an aircraft expects to start moving from its parking position at the airport (i.e., push back from the gate or start taxiing).
      • TOBT: Target Off-Block Time This is the time that an aircraft operator or ground handler has agreed upon with air traffic control or airport authorities for starting the off-block procedure. It's essentially the target time for when an aircraft should begin its departure process.
      • TSAT: Target Start Approval Time This is the time provided by air traffic control when an aircraft can expect to receive its start-up approval, which is crucial for sequencing departures at busy airports. It helps in managing the flow of traffic on the ground to avoid congestion.
      • CTOT: Calculated Take-Off Time Also known as “slot time,” this is the time calculated by air traffic control for an aircraft to take off. It's used in flow management to regulate the departure sequence, especially in scenarios where there's a need to balance demand with capacity at departure or arrival airports.
      • TTOT: Target Take-Off Time Similar to CTOT, this is the specific time at which an aircraft is scheduled to take off, considering all the previous steps like TOBT and TSAT have been coordinated. It's a target rather than an actual time of take-off, which might differ due to various operational factors.

      These abbreviations are part of the Airport Collaborative Decision Making (A-CDM).

      Hextory

      Hextory

      Select an aircraft on the map. ADD to HEXTORY reads ICAO hex, registration, type, and airline from the details card and adds it here, newest on top (12 aircraft plus the airport cards). Older cards drop off the bottom. FIND picks Registration, Airline, or Aircraft. Hex is typed in Registration. A full registration or hex adds a tail and isolates it when it is known; a parked tail still gets a Hextory card. Airline, aircraft, and the chips filter live traffic at the current airport. ↺ or FIND → CANCEL clears that filter. BACKUP LIST / RESTORE LIST brings a saved set back. Tap a card to find that aircraft on the map.

      Pin a Schiphol flight — it lands here and the map link is copied.

      The top-right cards are the most recently viewed airport and the one before it. Tap either to return there. They cannot be deleted or moved.

      The hourglass-eye on a card opens that registration’s Flightradar24 history in a new tab.

      FLYtification is on the phone only. Tap the bell to watch one aircraft. GearUp then notifies the next ADS-B events — first seen, takeoff, landing, and when it drops off or looks parked. Only one tail at a time (tapping another bell moves the watch). Tap the same bell again to turn it off. A watch ends after 24 hours. Add GearUp to the Home Screen and allow notifications so the short lock-screen line can appear (for example “PH-CKA is taking off · AMS → JRO”). The Mac does not show the bell or those alerts.

      The globe on the airport card opens that airport’s Flightradar24 page in a new tab.

      Swipe a card past the left edge of the screen to remove it. Swipe right past the right edge to copy the map link to your clipboard for sharing anywhere. Handy for your friends and family.

      BACKUP LIST saves this set. RESTORE LIST brings that save back.
